Why this one

Two things nothing else in the category does.

Ticker in, not data in.

Almost every tool like this takes pasted rows or an uploaded CSV. You type AAPL and the fetch does the rest — prices, splits, the date range, the moving averages already warm on the first bar drawn.

For someone covering markets every week, that is the whole difference between a tool you use and a tool you keep meaning to use.

The thumbnail comes out of the same render.

Save this frame writes whatever the preview is showing as a full-resolution PNG — same chart, same moment, same size as the video.

The thumbnail is not a side quest for anyone publishing this stuff. Shipping it in the same pass as the clip is worth more than it sounds.

How it goes

Ticker to finished clip, in about a minute of your attention.

Pick the chart and the symbols

One ticker or eight, a date-range preset or your own dates, daily bars or five-minute ones.

Scrub the preview until it looks right

A live frame from anywhere in the animation, redrawn as you change things. No render needed to see the composition.

Render, and take the thumbnail with you

Progress by the frame while it works. An MP4 at the end, plus the still if you want it.

What's in it

Broadcast-ready straight out of the renderer.

Every default is chosen so the file you get can go on a timeline untouched. If a feature would need you to fix it up afterwards, it isn't finished.

  • {{ themes }} themes — colour lives in one place, never hardcoded into a chart.
  • 16:9, 9:16 and 1:1 — landscape, shorts and square from the same config.
  • Transparent ProRes 4444 — real alpha for overlaying on footage, not a matte to key.
  • Four camera moves — push in, pull back, follow the line, or locked off.
  • Log price axes — equal percentage moves at equal height, with moving averages.
  • Up to 1440p at 60fps — draft tier for checking, max for publishing.
  • Intraday intervals — five-minute bars on their own position axis, so overnight gaps don't eat the frame.
  • Brand kits — theme, footer and title template, saved between runs.

Straight answer

Today it runs on your machine.

What that gets you

Your renders are yours — unlimited, unwatermarked, at full quality, never queued behind anyone. Nothing phones home and the price cache is a folder you can delete.

ffmpeg ships with it, so pip install -r requirements.txt and python app.py is the whole setup.

What it costs you

That is still a Python install, and the honest read is that it is the step most people stop at. A packaged one-click build is next, and hosted rendering after that.

The link at the top runs the real interface in your browser with no install at all, so you can find out whether it's worth the trouble first.
